Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

The short version: Cadence runs entirely on your Mac (and your iPhone, if you use the companion app). It has no servers, no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. The developer never receives your calendar data or anything else about you.

Who we are

Cadence is a macOS menu-bar app with an optional iPhone companion app, developed by Patrick McKinley ("we", "us"). This policy covers both apps and explains what information they use and where it goes.

Information Cadence uses

To do its job, Cadence reads the following on your devices. None of it is sent to us. Unless a row says otherwise, the iPhone app keeps the same things in the same way on the phone.

Services Cadence connects to

Cadence connects only to services you choose to use, directly from your Mac:

  • Google: only if you sign in with Google. Cadence talks to Google's sign-in service and the Google Calendar API directly from your Mac. Google's handling of your data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Microsoft: only if you sign in with Microsoft. Cadence talks to Microsoft's sign-in service and Microsoft Graph directly from your Mac. Microsoft's handling of your data is covered by the Microsoft Privacy Statement.
  • Slack: only if you connect a workspace for running-late messages. Cadence talks to Slack's sign-in service and the Slack API directly from your Mac. See Slack's Privacy Policy.
  • Your iPhone and Mac: the two apps talk to each other directly, never through a server. See Cadence for iPhone below.
  • AI assistants you connect: only if you set one up in Settings → Assistants. See below.
  • ICS / webcal feed hosts — whatever calendar subscription URLs you add.
  • Apple Maps — when estimating travel time, Cadence sends the meeting location and your approximate position to Apple's MapKit service. See Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Meeting services: when you click Join, the meeting link opens in your browser (in the profile you picked, if any) or the relevant app (Zoom, Teams, etc.).

Calendars you've added in macOS Calendar are read locally through Apple's EventKit framework; Cadence doesn't contact those providers itself.

Running-late messages

Running-late messages are off until you allow them for an account in Settings → Accounts. That asks Google, Microsoft or Slack for the extra permissions listed below.

  • Only when you click. A message is sent only when you press Send (or choose Send Running Late). It goes from your own account, to the place shown on the button: a meeting chat, a direct or group message, a Slack channel or an email to the attendees.
  • Checking where it can go. To show which options will work, Cadence asks that service about the meeting's attendees before you send, for example looking up their email addresses in Slack, finding the Teams meeting chat, or finding an existing Google Chat conversation. These lookups send nothing to anyone.
  • Nothing kept. Cadence doesn't keep a copy of the messages it sends. They are stored by Google, Microsoft or Slack like any message you send yourself.

Google user data

When you choose Sign in with Google, Cadence asks for these permissions and nothing else:

  • openid, email and profile: your Google account's email address and profile photo, used only to label the account in Settings so you can tell your accounts apart.
  • calendar.readonly: read access to your calendars and events, used only to show your meetings, countdowns, join links, reminders, clash prompts and pending invites inside the app.
  • calendar.events: used only for two things you trigger yourself: sending your Accept, Maybe or Decline reply to an invite, and changing an event's colour. Cadence never creates or deletes events and never changes anything else about them.

If you allow running-late messages for a Google account, Cadence also asks for these. They are only used to send the messages you choose to send:

  • gmail.send: send a running-late email from your account. Cadence can't read your email.
  • chat.spaces.readonly, chat.spaces.create and chat.messages.create: find or start a Google Chat conversation with the meeting's attendees and post your message in it.

This data goes straight from Google to the app on your Mac. It never passes through or is stored on any server we run, and it isn't shared with anyone. It isn't used for advertising or to train AI or machine-learning models, and no person reads it.

Cadence's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

You can disconnect an account at any time in Cadence → Settings → Accounts. Disconnecting revokes the token with Google and removes it and the cached photo from your Mac.

Microsoft user data

When you choose Sign in with Microsoft, Cadence asks for these Microsoft Graph permissions:

  • openid, email, profile and User.Read: your account's email address and profile photo, used only to label the account in Settings.
  • offline_access: keeps you signed in between launches.
  • Calendars.ReadWrite: read your calendars and events to show them in the app, and send your Accept, Maybe or Decline reply to an invite. Cadence never creates, edits or deletes events.
  • MailboxSettings.Read: read your Outlook category colours so events show in the same colours as in Outlook. Cadence doesn't read your email.

If you allow running-late messages for a Microsoft account, Cadence also asks for these. They are only used to send the messages you choose to send:

  • Mail.Send: send a running-late email from your account.
  • ChatMessage.Send and OnlineMeetings.Read (work and school accounts only): find a Teams meeting's chat and post your message in it.

As with Google, this data goes straight from Microsoft to the app on your Mac, is never sent to us or shared with anyone, and isn't used for advertising or to train AI models. Disconnecting an account in Settings → Accounts deletes its token and cached photo from your Mac.

Slack user data

When you connect a Slack workspace, Cadence asks only for these user permissions, so messages are posted as you:

  • chat:write: post your running-late message.
  • users:read and users:read.email: find the meeting's attendees in Slack by their email address.
  • im:write: open a direct message with them.

Slack only lets apps redirect sign-ins to HTTPS addresses, so at the end of signing in Slack sends your browser to cadence.patrick-mckinley.com/slack. That page immediately hands the result to Cadence on your Mac. It doesn't store or send it anywhere else, and the one-time code it carries is useless without a secret that never leaves your Mac. Like any website, the page is served by GitHub Pages, which may keep standard request logs under GitHub's Privacy Statement.

Disconnecting a workspace in Settings → Accounts revokes the token with Slack and deletes it from your Mac.

Cadence for iPhone

The iPhone app works on its own or paired with Cadence on your Mac. On its own, it reads its own calendars (iPhone Calendar, Google, Microsoft 365 and ICS feeds) exactly as described above, using the same Google and Microsoft permissions, minus the running-late ones.

  • Permissions it asks for. Calendars, to show your meetings. Notifications, for alerts while the app is in the background. Local Network, to reach your Mac. The Camera, only to scan the pairing code on your Mac; nothing it sees is saved.
  • Pairing. The pairing code holds a random 256-bit key that both devices keep in their Keychain and use to encrypt everything between them (TLS with a pre-shared key). The phone finds the Mac over Bonjour on your network, or directly between the two devices when they aren't on the same Wi-Fi. Nothing goes through the internet or any server of ours.
  • What travels between them. Your meetings for today and tomorrow, alerts, reminders, invites and clash prompts from the Mac, and what you tap on the phone (Join, Snooze, Dismiss, invite replies) back to the Mac.
  • Live Activities in the Dynamic Island and on the Lock Screen are drawn on the phone. Cadence doesn't use Apple's push service for them.
  • Unpairing. Forget All iPhones on the Mac changes the key, so no phone can connect until it's paired again. Unpairing on the phone deletes the Mac's saved agenda.

AI assistants

Cadence includes an optional local server that AI assistants on your Mac (such as Claude, Cursor or Windsurf) can use through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). Nothing uses it until you connect an assistant, and you can turn it off completely in Settings → Assistants.

  • Local only. Assistants reach Cadence through a Unix socket in your Application Support folder that only your user account can open. There is no network port, so nothing else on your network or the internet can connect.
  • What they can see. A connected assistant can read the same merged calendar you see in Cadence (events, invites, clashes, free time, your calendar list) and whether you're presenting or on a call.
  • Where it goes next. What an assistant reads is handled by that assistant and its provider, under their own privacy policy, and may be sent to their servers. Only connect assistants you trust.
  • You approve actions. Replying to invites, changing colours, joining meetings, hiding events and snoozing alerts each show a card in Cadence first. Requests you don't answer within a minute are denied. You can choose to auto-approve specific actions.
  • Setup changes. When you click Install, Cadence adds a cadence entry to that assistant's MCP configuration file and keeps a backup of the previous version. It doesn't change anything else in the file.

What we don't do

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or advertising.
  • No accounts and no Cadence servers.
  • We don't sell, share or receive any of your data.

Your choices

  • Calendar and location access can be granted or revoked any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security on the Mac, and calendar, camera and local network access in Settings → Privacy & Security on the iPhone.
  • Disconnect Google, Microsoft or Slack accounts, or remove ICS feeds, in Cadence → Settings → Accounts. You can also revoke access from your account: Google at myaccount.google.com/permissions, Microsoft at account.live.com/consent/Manage (personal accounts) or myapps.microsoft.com (work or school accounts), Slack under your workspace's Manage apps page.
  • Deleting the app, its preferences and its Application Support folder removes everything Cadence stores. Its Keychain items can be removed in Keychain Access (search for com.cadence.calendarmonitor). Deleting the iPhone app removes everything it stores on the phone.

Children

Cadence isn't directed at children and doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone.
