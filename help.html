Help & setup guides

The fiddly bits, step by step

Most of Cadence works the moment you grant calendar access. These guides cover the settings that need a little more: a developer app of your own, a feed link to find, or a permission to grant.

First launch: a quick setup

Mac1 minute

The first time Cadence opens, a short setup walks you through the basics:

  1. Welcome. An arrow points at Cadence's icon in the menu bar, where it lives from now on.
  2. Calendars. Connect macOS Calendar, Google, Microsoft 365 or an ICS link, and untick the calendars you don't want alerts for, like holidays and birthdays. macOS asks for calendar access here, not before.
  3. How loud. Gentle (a notification 2 minutes before, nothing full screen), Normal (a full-screen alert 30 seconds before) or Never let me be late (a notification 5 minutes before, then a full-screen alert a minute before that stays until you act). Try a test alert before you choose.
  4. Stay running. Open Cadence at login, and pick how it stays up to date.
  5. Done. Your next meeting, and shortcuts to the extras: browser profiles, running-late messages, working hours, music and AI assistants.

Skip anything you like. A calendar not connected yet or notifications turned off wait in the menu as a Finish setting up checklist, which you can dismiss.

To see it again, choose Settings → General → Run Setup Again. It starts from your current settings and resets nothing. If launch at login fails, setup explains why and opens System Settings → Login Items.

Keeping Cadence up to date

MacNo setup

Choose a mode during setup or in Settings → General → Updates:

  • Tell me about updates (recommended): Cadence checks a few times a day and lets you know when a new version is out. One click updates and restarts it.
  • Update automatically: new versions download and install in the background. Cadence only restarts when no meeting is on or starting within 10 minutes.
  • Don't check for updates: Cadence never contacts GitHub. Check Now in Settings still works when you want it.

Updates come from Cadence's GitHub releases, stable versions only. Before installing, Cadence checks each download against the release's SHA-256 checksums and checks that the new app is signed with the same Apple Developer ID. If either check fails, it doesn't install.

What's New in the menu, in Settings and on the update notification opens a window listing each change since your version, marked Breaking, New, Fix or Other. It also opens by itself the first time a new version runs.

Cadence needs to be able to write to the folder it's in to update itself, so keep it in Applications. Builds you made yourself aren't signed with the Developer ID, so they offer a download link instead.

Spotify on the iPhone: bring your own Client ID

iPhoneAbout 3 minutesFree

To show and control Spotify on the desk display, Cadence talks to the Spotify app through Spotify's App Remote SDK, which needs a Client ID from a Spotify developer app. Spotify only lets a new developer app work for the people its owner adds, so one app can't be shared with everyone. Instead you make your own. It's free and you only do it once.

  1. On the iPhone, open Cadence's Settings → Music & Media, pick Spotify as the media app, and tap Track details. Keep this screen open: it has the two values you'll paste below, each with a copy button.
  2. Open the Spotify Developer Dashboard, sign in with your Spotify account and choose Create app. Any name and description will do, like My Cadence.
  3. Under Redirect URIs, add: 
    cadence-companion://spotify-callback
  4. Under Which API/SDKs are you planning to use?, tick iOS and add the Bundle ID shown on Cadence's Spotify app screen.
  5. Save, open the app's Settings page in the dashboard and copy its Client ID (32 letters and numbers).
  6. Paste it into the Client ID field in Cadence and tap Save. Then tap Connect on the desk display: Spotify opens for a moment to ask you to approve, then hands you back.
Good to know
  • There's no Client Secret. The Client ID isn't a secret, and it stays on your iPhone.
  • Your new app stays in Spotify's development mode, which is fine: it works for the account that made it. To use it on someone else's account, add them under User Management in the dashboard.
  • The Spotify app has to be installed on the phone, and running, for the desk display to connect.
  • Saving a different Client ID signs you out of Spotify in Cadence. Connect again afterwards.
  • Opening Spotify and pausing it from the desk work without any of this. The Client ID only adds track details and artwork.
  • Apple Music needs none of this: allow access to your music when the phone asks, which it does the first time you pick Apple Music or open it from the desk.

Google Calendar: your own OAuth client

MacAbout 5 minutesOptional

If Sign in with Google works in your copy of Cadence, you don't need this. It's for building Cadence yourself, or for signing in through a Google Cloud project you control (for example, if your company only allows its own apps).

  1. In Google Cloud Console, create a project (project picker → New project, for example Cadence) and select it.
  2. Enable the API: APIs & Services → Library → Google Calendar API → Enable.
  3. Consent screen: Google Auth Platform → Get started. App name Cadence, your email as the support contact, then pick an Audience:
    • Internal for Google Workspace accounts in your own domain. Simplest: no test users, and sign-ins don't expire.
    • External for personal @gmail.com accounts or more than one domain. Read the warning below.
  4. (Optional) Data Access → Add or remove scopes: add calendar.readonly and calendar.events. Cadence asks for both anyway; adding them makes the consent screen accurate.
  5. Create the client: Clients → Create client, type Desktop app. Copy the Client ID and Client secret. You don't need a redirect URI.
  6. In Cadence on the Mac, open Settings → Accounts → Use my own OAuth client…, paste both values, then click Sign in with Google. The browser tab says "Cadence is connected" when it's done.
External apps expire sign-ins after 7 days

New External apps start in Testing mode: you must add every account under Audience → Test users (or sign-in fails with "Access blocked"), and Google drops the sign-in after a week, so Cadence shows Reconnect. To stop that, click Publish app under Audience. You don't need Google's verification for personal use. Sign-in then warns "Google hasn't verified this app": choose Advanced → Go to Cadence (unsafe). That's expected, because the app is your own.

Microsoft 365: your own app registration

MacAbout 3 minutesOptional

Again, only if Sign in with Microsoft doesn't work out of the box, or your organisation wants its own registration.

  1. In the Microsoft Entra admin center (or Azure portal → App registrations), choose New registration. Name it Cadence. For account types, pick Accounts in any organizational directory and personal Microsoft accounts, or just your organisation.
  2. Redirect URI: platform Public client/native (mobile & desktop), with: 
    http://localhost
    Microsoft accepts any port on localhost, and Cadence picks one when you sign in.
  3. Register and copy the Application (client) ID. There's no secret: Cadence signs in with PKCE.
  4. (Optional) API permissions → Add → Microsoft Graph → Delegated: Calendars.ReadWrite, MailboxSettings.Read, User.Read, offline_access. Some organisations need an admin to approve these first.
  5. Paste the ID into Cadence's Settings → Accounts → Use my own app registration… and sign in.

Calendars.ReadWrite is only used to send your invite replies. Cadence never creates, edits or deletes events. MailboxSettings.Read reads your Outlook category colours.

Calendar feeds (ICS / webcal)

Mac and iPhone1 minute

Any calendar with an iCalendar link can go straight into Cadence: team rotas, holidays, sports fixtures, or a calendar you can't sign in to directly. On the Mac it's Settings → Accounts → Subscribed calendars; paste the link and you're done.

  • Google Calendar: on the web, open Settings → Settings for my calendars → your calendar → Integrate calendar and copy the Secret address in iCal format.
  • Outlook: Settings → Calendar → Shared calendars → Publish a calendar, pick the calendar and Can view all details, then copy the ICS link.
  • Everything else: look for "Subscribe", "Export", "iCal" or a webcal:// link. Both webcal:// and https:// work.

Treat secret addresses like passwords: anyone with the link can read the calendar. Feeds are read-only, so you can't reply to invites from them; sign in to Google or Microsoft directly for that.

Running-late messages

Mac2 minutes

Once a meeting has started, the alert can send a quick "running late" message from you. It's off until you allow it, account by account.

  1. In Settings → Accounts, turn on running-late messages for a Google or Microsoft account. Cadence asks for the extra permissions it needs to send mail and chat messages (see the privacy policy for exactly which).
  2. To use Slack, connect your workspace in the same place. Messages are sent as you, not as a bot.
  3. Add domain rules to pick a channel by who's in the meeting, like @client.com → Slack. Without a rule, Cadence tries the meeting's own chat first (Teams meeting chat, Google Chat for Meet, the Slack huddle's channel), then email.
  4. Email from Google: Cadence's built-in Google sign-in can post to Google Chat but not send Gmail, so email goes from a Microsoft account if you have one. To send Gmail too, use your own Google OAuth client (see above) with the Gmail API turned on.
  5. Edit the suggested messages if you like. Cadence picks one based on what came before: wrapping up the last meeting, a breather between back-to-backs, or just running behind.

Opening meetings in the right browser profile

Mac2 minutes

Signed in to different Google or Microsoft accounts in different browser profiles? Cadence can open each meeting in the right one. It works with Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi and Firefox (including Firefox containers) as your default browser.

  • Per account or calendar: Settings → Calendars, then pick a profile next to each one. Cadence matches profiles to your accounts by email where it can.
  • By organiser: Settings → Browser, then add rules like @client.com → Client profile.
  • One meeting or series: right-click Join → Join in.
  • Apps vs. browser: choose per service whether an assigned profile beats the Zoom or Teams app.

With Safari as your default browser, links open as usual.

Working hours and breaks

Mac and iPhone2 minutes

Set your hours for each weekday and the breaks you want kept clear (lunch, a walk, the school run) in Settings → Work on the Mac. Each of these defences can be switched on or off:

  • Invites that land on a break, outside your hours or on a day off say so on the invite card, the notification and the iPhone pop-up.
  • Assistants only get free time inside your hours, with breaks kept out of find_free_time.
  • A nudge when a break starts, and a warning five minutes before if your current meeting is going to run into it.
  • A meeting booked over a break asks you, like a double-booking: keep the break (the meeting won't alert) or give it up for the day.

The iPhone uses the Mac's schedule while they're paired, or its own (Settings → Working Hours & Breaks) when it's on its own.

Pairing, and several Macs or iPhones

Mac and iPhone1 minute each

  1. On the Mac, open Settings → iPhone → Show Pairing Code.
  2. Scan it with Cadence on the iPhone, or with the Camera app, or copy the link across.
  3. That's it. They find each other on your Wi-Fi, or directly when you're not on the same network.
  • More iPhones on one Mac: pair each one the same way. Alerts and prompts go to all of them, and the Mac's media keys follow whichever went on display last.
  • One iPhone, several Macs: on the phone, tap Pair Another Mac under your Macs at the top of Settings. Agendas are merged, so a meeting on both Macs shows once.
  • Which Mac opens Join: the highest one in the list that has the meeting. Tap Edit to drag them into order. Turn on Open web links on the first Mac to send any plain web link to your top Mac, even if only another Mac has the meeting.
  • Removing a phone: on the Mac, Settings → iPhone → Forget… next to it deletes just that phone's key. Forget All iPhones unpairs them all.
  • What's going over the link: open a paired device (on the Mac, click it in Settings → iPhone; on the phone, tap the Mac in Settings) and turn on Advanced for data used, messages by type over time and the last 50 messages (their type and size, not their contents). It's kept in memory only and gone when the app quits.
  • Codes are single-use: each code works once, for 15 minutes, and showing a new one cancels the last. A pairing link opened from Safari, Messages or the Camera app asks before pairing.

Let the Mac's media keys control the phone

Mac1 minuteOptional

  1. On the Mac, turn on Settings → Alerts → Media remote control.
  2. macOS asks for Accessibility access. Allow Cadence in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Cadence only watches the media keys, never your typing.
  3. Choose when the mini-player shows: never, on key presses, or always.

The keys only go to the phone while it shows the desk display. They stay with the Mac during calls, when the phone hasn't been heard from for a few seconds, and (adjustable, or off) once the phone has been paused for 30 seconds. If another app also grabs the media keys, Settings lists it so you can see what's getting in the way.

AI assistants

Mac1 minute

  1. Open Settings → Assistants. Cadence finds Claude, Claude Code, Cursor, Windsurf, Gemini CLI and Antigravity on your Mac and shows whether each is set up.
  2. Click Install, then let Cadence restart the app so it picks up the new server. Command-line tools like Claude Code and Gemini CLI pick it up in their next session. If Cadence isn't in your Applications folder, it offers to move itself there first so the path keeps working.
  3. Try a prompt from the automation catalog.
  • Other apps: any MCP client that runs local servers can use Cadence; the automations page has the config to copy. The Gemini desktop app only takes remote servers, so it can't connect.
  • Approvals: every action shows an Allow / Deny card. Turn on auto-approval for specific actions in the same tab.
  • Notes on meetings: switch assistant attachments off here if you'd rather not have them. On the Mac, the Notes button lives in Settings → General; on the iPhone, in Settings → Desk Mode.
  • Activity: see every call from the last 30 days, filter by assistant, or clear it.

Troubleshooting

The site says Cadence is "in testing". What does that mean?
Cadence's built-in Sign in with Google isn't public yet: Google is still reviewing it, and until it approves, only accounts on the test list can use it. Everyone else sees "Access blocked". You don't have to wait. Create your own Google OAuth client (about 5 minutes, free) and paste it into Settings → Accounts → Use my own OAuth client…. The rest of Cadence works as normal, including calendars from macOS Calendar and ICS feeds. Or volunteer for testing.
Can I volunteer for testing?
Yes! I'm happy to add a small number of people to the testing client. Message me :) Include the Google account you'd sign in with, and I'll add it to the test list.
Google says "Access blocked".
Your own client is in Testing mode and the account isn't a test user. Add it under Audience → Test users, or publish the app (see Google).
A Google account keeps asking me to reconnect every week.
Same cause: Testing-mode sign-ins last 7 days. Publish the app.
macOS keeps asking Cadence for Keychain access.
This happens after rebuilding Cadence yourself. Choose Always Allow.
Spotify won't connect on the desk display.
Check that the Spotify app is installed and has been opened, that your developer app has the exact redirect URI and the Bundle ID from Cadence's screen, and that you're signed in to Spotify with the account that made the app (or one added under User Management).
Invite replies or colours don't work for an older account.
Accounts connected before replies existed are read-only. Click Reconnect in Settings → Accounts.
My assistant can't see Cadence.
Restart the assistant app after installing. If you've moved Cadence since, install again so the path is right. Apps that only connect to remote MCP servers, like the Gemini desktop app, can't reach it; it never opens a network port. Use Gemini CLI or Antigravity instead.
Cadence says it can't update itself.
Move it to Applications (it needs to replace itself in place), then click Check Now in Settings → General. Or download the new version from the downloads page.
The iPhone can't find the Mac.
Make sure Cadence is running on the Mac and that the phone allowed Local Network access (iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network).
An account isn't behaving itself.
If a Google, Microsoft 365 or Slack account is acting up (missing or stale meetings, invite replies or colours that won't save, running-late messages that won't send, sign-in prompts that keep coming back), sign out and in again. That gives it a fresh sign-in with every permission Cadence needs:
  1. Open Settings → Accounts and click Sign Out next to the account. Its calendars disappear from Cadence for now.
  2. Sign in again with Sign in with Google, Sign in with Microsoft or Sign in with Slack (or Add Another Account if you have more than one). Pick the same account, and allow everything it asks for.
  3. Check its calendars are ticked in Settings → Calendars, and switch running-late messages back on for it if you use them.
On the iPhone, the same Sign Out is in the app's Settings, next to the account.
